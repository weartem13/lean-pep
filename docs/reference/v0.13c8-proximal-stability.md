## 1. Firm proximal maps

Adding two global support inequalities gives

    <s-t, x-y> >= 0.

This uses the two supplied supports, not a separate convexity hypothesis.
For positive L and convex real-valued h, C5 optimality supplies
s=L(v-p), t=L(w-q) for actual prox outputs p,q. Cancelling positive L gives

    ||p-q||^2 <= <p-q,v-w>.

The equivalent energy consequence is

    ||p-q||^2 + ||(v-w)-(p-q)||^2 <= ||v-w||^2.

C6's selected map inherits this inequality. Both proxMap and I-proxMap are
1-Lipschitz, and proxMap is continuous. The physical subgradient residual
L*(I-proxMap) is NOT being claimed 1-Lipschitz independently of L.
These conditional theorems need HasProx but no completeness, finite dimension
or continuity of h. The supplied-solution theorems do not even need HasProx.

## 2. The full physical step

Adding the old two ordered normalized smooth interpolation inequalities proves

    ||G(x)-G(y)||^2 <= <G(x)-G(y),x-y>.

The original ParametricGDScaling.normalizedFirstOrder supplies G=g/L for a
positive smoothness bound L. Thus F(x)=x-g(x)/L is firmly nonexpansive too.
Write T(x)=proxMap(F(x)). Combining the two firm energy inequalities gives

    ||T(x)-T(y)||^2 + (1/2)||[x-y]-[T(x)-T(y)]||^2 <= ||x-y||^2.

The coefficient 1/2 is the standard energy form associated with a
2/3-averaged map. No bundled averaged-operator definition is added here.
The source proves the displayed inequality and 1-Lipschitz continuity.
It does NOT claim the composition itself is firm.

For example, L=1, f(x,y)=x^2/2 and h(x,y)=3(x+y)^2/2 give

    F = diag(0,1),   proxMap = (1/7) [[4,-3],[-3,4]].

These maps do not commute. On d=(1,1), T(d)=(-3/7,4/7): its squared norm
25/49 exceeds <T(d),d>=1/7. The residual coefficient one would fail, whereas
coefficient 1/2 satisfies the new inequality. This finite-dimensional
counterexample is checked exactly in Python; it is not a separate Lean
counterexample theorem.

The existing C5 minimizer/fixed-point equivalence identifies fixed points of
this precise T with ordinary global minimizers of f+h. Separate stationarity
of the two components is never required.

## 3. Same canonical trajectory, new energy estimates

For x_k=iterate hp g x0 k and every supplied composite minimizer xStar,

    ||x_(k+1)-xStar||^2 + (1/2)||x_(k+1)-x_k||^2 <= ||x_k-xStar||^2.

Finite telescoping (including N=0) gives

    ||x_N-xStar||^2 + (1/2) sum_(k=0)^(N-1) ||x_(k+1)-x_k||^2
      <= ||x0-xStar||^2.

The step norms are nonincreasing by nonexpansiveness of T. Therefore

    ||x_(N+1)-x_N||^2 <= 2||x0-xStar||^2/(N+1).

The indexing here is actual iteration index N, including N=0; it is not the
PEP compiler's n with T=n+1. The bound controls the step FROM x_N TO x_(N+1).
The constant two is a derived upper bound, not asserted sharp.

A squeeze argument followed by continuity of sqrt proves

    ||x_(k+1)-x_k|| -> 0.

This is asymptotic regularity, NOT a proof that x_k converges in norm or weakly,
NOT a claim that it reaches a minimizer after finitely many steps, and NOT a
summable-errors convergence theorem. The proof only needs the finite energy
estimates and a minimizer; it does not reuse C7's Cauchy minimizing sequence
as if it were the outer algorithm.

For two arbitrary initial points, even without a minimizer,

    ||iterate(x0,N)-iterate(y0,N)|| <= ||x0-y0||.

Hilbert wrappers use C7's actual existence proof to remove HasProx from the
caller's hypotheses. They explicitly keep CompleteSpace and Continuous h.

## 4. Approximate trajectories and gradient error

For an arbitrary sequence z and pointwise distances to the same exact map,

    ||z_(k+1)-T(z_k)|| <= eps_k,  k<N,

induction and the triangle inequality prove

    ||z_N-x_N|| <= ||z_0-x0|| + sum_(k=0)^(N-1) eps_k.

The per-step hypotheses already force eps_k >= 0 on the prefix; no unused
global nonnegativity hypothesis is added. Values after N are unconstrained.
This is a distance-to-exact estimate, not an objective-gap guarantee.

For exact prox solves with an additive gradient error e_k,

    z_(k+1)=proxMap(z_k-(g(z_k)+e_k)/L),

the same prox nonexpansiveness gives eps_k=||e_k||/L and thus

    ||z_N-x_N|| <= ||z_0-x0|| + sum_(k=0)^(N-1) ||e_k||/L.

No continuity or boundedness of gradients of h is inferred. The old exact
LR^2/(4T) objective theorem is not applied to this perturbed sequence.
