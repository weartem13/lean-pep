## Actual mathematical scope

Let E be a real Hilbert space, L>0, f,g satisfy the existing smooth-convex
contract, h be continuous, convex, real-valued and full-domain, and f+h have
a global minimizer. P is the unchanged proxMap selected using C7 existence.
The physical reference step is exactly the C8 expression

    T(x) = P(x - g(x)/L).

The same infinite sequence u must satisfy for every natural n

    ||u(n+1) - T(u(n))|| <= eps(n),   Summable eps.

Nonnegativity of eps is implied by these norm bounds. Then the new proof
scripts give boundedness, a finite limit of the distance to every minimizer,
vanishing exact-map residuals, vanishing adjacent steps, and weak convergence
to some composite minimizer. The public weak limit uses mathlib WeakSpace.
The first-order conditional version retains the original HasProx proof and
does not add continuity of h. Finite dimension gives a separate strong
convergence theorem, using a compact norm ball, without a continuity premise
on the full-domain convex h or a HasProx premise in its analytic wrapper.

This is a sufficient error condition, not a claimed necessary condition.
Summable norms are not the same as a conditionally convergent vector series,
vanishing individual errors, or square-summable errors.

## 1. Scalar tail correction

`SummablePerturbation.lean` defines only a scalar bookkeeping function:

    errorTail eps n = (sum' k, eps k) - sum_{k<n} eps k.

Its nonnegativity and limit-zero theorems explicitly require summability.
Its successor identity is the finite-sum algebraic identity

    tail(n+1) = tail(n) - eps(n).

For nonnegative a(n) with a(n+1) <= a(n)+eps(n), the corrected potential

    b(n) = a(n) + tail(n)

is antitone and nonnegative. Monotone convergence applies to b, while
summability gives tail(n)->0, hence a(n) has a finite limit. The uncorrected
sequence a is not claimed antitone.

For an inexact nonexpansive orbit and any fixed point p, the triangle
inequality gives the required scalar recurrence for a(n)=||u(n)-p||.
The resulting uniform bound is

    ||u(n)-p|| <= B := ||u(0)-p|| + sum' n, eps(n).

The tail correction is only a proof device. It is not an online-computable
stopping rule or a change to the algorithm.

## 2. Positive energy margin and residual convergence

Nonexpansiveness alone does not force residual convergence. The abstract
operator theorem additionally requires a positive constant c and

    ||T(x)-p||^2 + c ||T(x)-x||^2 <= ||x-p||^2

at each fixed point. C8's existing forward-backward energy inequality
provides exactly c=1/2; neither the prox map nor the step size is changed.

Write r(n)=||T(u(n))-u(n)|| and d(n)=||u(n)-p||. Perturbing the next point
gives a pointwise bound with all terms retained:

    c r(n)^2 <= d(n)^2-d(n+1)^2 + 2 B eps(n) + eps(n)^2.

The two squared-distance terms converge to the same scalar limit.
Summability implies eps(n)->0. Divide by positive c and squeeze to get
r(n)^2->0; nonnegativity and continuity of sqrt give r(n)->0. Finally,

    ||u(n+1)-u(n)|| <= eps(n)+r(n)

also tends to zero. These two residual sequences are not definitionally
equal and neither is asserted antitone. No explicit asymptotic rate or
new finite energy sharp constant is claimed.

## 3. Reuse C10 weak compactness and demiclosedness

`WeakQuasiFejerConvergence.lean` uses the unchanged C10 weakRiesz closed
balls, nontrivial cluster filters, bounded demiclosedness, standard
WeakSpace conversion, and distance-square-difference theorem.

Only the scalar hypothesis in the Opial uniqueness argument is generalized:
existence of the distance limits to both candidate points replaces their
exact antitonicity. The difference of squared distances along the two
nontrivial tail filters still has limits -||z-w||^2 and +||z-w||^2. These
must coincide, so z=w. Weak compactness plus unique cluster point then gives
full weak convergence. No weak continuity of the nonlinear map, separability,
first countability or weak sequential compactness is assumed.

Norm strong convergence is proved separately when a compact set contains
the orbit: C9 identifies a convergent subsequence's limit as a fixed point;
the full distance limit to it must be zero. Finite dimension makes the
error-enlarged closed ball compact. This is not a weak-to-strong inference.

## 4. Actual proximal-gradient interfaces

`ProximalGradientInexactConvergence.lean` refers to the original
`forwardBackwardStep hp g` throughout. It adds no recursive definition and
no new prox selection. The central statements are:

- `inexact_distance_has_limit`, `inexact_norm_sub_le_budget`;
- `inexact_residual_tendsto_zero`, `inexact_step_norm_tendsto_zero`;
- `inexact_weak_tendsto`, `inexact_weak_tendsto_hilbert`;
- `inexact_tendsto_finiteDimensional`, its `_analytic` wrapper;
- `inexact_weak_tendsto_of_unique`;
- `oracle_prox_error_bound`, `inexact_oracle_prox_weak_tendsto`,
  its `_hilbert` wrapper, and `inexact_gradient_weak_tendsto`.

For simultaneous gradient and prox-output errors, the actual contract is

    ||u(n+1) - P(u(n) - (g(u(n))+e(n))/L)|| <= delta(n),
    Summable (fun n => ||e(n)||),    Summable delta.

C8's oracle-error bound and the triangle inequality give the combined budget

    eps(n) = delta(n) + ||e(n)||/L.

This is norm error of the approximate prox OUTPUT, not a proximal subproblem
objective gap. A raw objective tolerance cannot be substituted for delta;
no such conversion theorem is supplied in this version.

## 5. Scope exclusions and counterexample diagnostics

Summable errors can change which minimizer is reached. For f=h=0 the exact
map is identity: an initial zero point followed by one nonzero perturbation
converges to that new point, whereas the exact orbit stays at zero. A
separate uniqueness assumption is required to identify the limiting
minimizer with a supplied reference.

Zero initial radius does not imply an inexact orbit stays stationary.
The old LR^2/(4T) PEP bound is for exact runs; with f(x)=x^2/4, L=1, h=0,
x0=x*=0, a single output perturbation to x1=1 has positive objective gap
although the exact zero-radius bound is zero.

Vanishing errors or square-summability alone do not give this class-wide
conclusion: under the identity map, positive harmonic errors accumulate.
Nonexpansiveness alone also does not suffice even with zero errors: T=-I
has a nonconvergent two-cycle and fails every positive energy margin.

These examples in Python are exact finite diagnostics, not new Lean
counterexample theorems. They do not assert that summability is necessary
for every convergent inexact trajectory.

No noisy objective convergence, noisy finite-time rate, weak convergence
rate, infinite-dimensional norm convergence, metric projection formula,
strong convergence to the exact orbit limit, extended-real penalty, or
variable step size is added. No old certificate or checker is changed.
