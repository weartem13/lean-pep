## Physical convention

For L : NNReal, `ProximalScaling.IsProxPoint L h v x` is the ordinary
minimization statement

    h(x) + (L/2)||x-v||² <= h(y) + (L/2)||y-v||² for every y.

L is the INVERSE proximal step size. `IsSmoothProxRun N L h g x` requires
this property at v = x_k - L^{-1} g(x_k), output x_(k+1), for k<N.
There is no selected prox operator or additional existence axiom. At L=1,
the contract is proved equivalent to the existing C2 `IsProxRun`.
A zero-step run remains vacuous; the rate is only for positive step counts.

## Exact normalization

C4 reuses `SmoothScaling.scaledPoint`, `normalizedFunction`,
`normalizedGradient`, and `normalizedFirstOrder`. For L>0, R>0,

    T(z) = xStar + R z,
    F(z) = (f(T(z))-f(xStar))/(LR²),
    H(z) = (h(T(z))-h(xStar))/(LR²),
    G(z) = g(T(z))/(LR),
    z_k = (x_k-xStar)/R.

Convexity of h and its supporting subgradients are transported separately
from the smooth first-order pair. A physical supporting vector s becomes
s/(LR), not s/(LR²).

The whole prox objective has the exact equality

    H(z) + (1/2)||z-v||²
      = [h(T(z)) + (L/2)||T(z)-T(v)||² - h(xStar)]/(LR²).

`normalized_prox_iff` proves BOTH directions of minimizer transport. Its
reverse direction uses inverse coordinates for every comparison point, not
just the finite samples. Neither convexity nor stationarity is needed for
this minimizer equivalence itself.

`isSmoothProxRun_iff_normalized` proves the physical and normalized finite
runs equivalent. Both function components use their own reference value;
independent additive offsets do not affect the normalized objective gap.

## Zero radius

No theorem with R=0 divides by R. A supporting subgradient at a reference
prox point, plus positive quadratic coefficient L, implies uniqueness of
any competing prox minimizer, by the existing exact quadratic remainder.
With the composite stationarity certificate -g(xStar) in partial h(xStar),
this proves that a supplied run starting at xStar stays there. In particular,
g(xStar) need not be zero. Radius zero follows from the radius inequality,
not from a special-case assumption on the component functions.

## Explicit physical lower witness

For T=n+1, use the OLD C3A slope and point sequence to define

    a = R/(2T),
    f(x) = L*x,      g(x) = L,
    h(x) = L*(a*max(x,0)-x),
    x_k = R*(1-k/(2T)).

For k<=T the points are nonnegative. At each output, the physical residual
subgradient is L*(a-1); the forward/prox update is exact. The finite prefix
is therefore a genuine physical prox run. The final point is R/2 and its
gap is exactly LR²/(4T). At R=0 all points are zero, but g(0)=L and the
nonsmooth supporting vector is -L. Thus the lower family also tests
nonzero component cancellation in the zero-radius case.

At L=R=1, all four witness definitions agree with C3A. At general positive R,
the normalized component split is f_norm(z)=z/R and
h_norm(z)=max(z,0)/(2T)-z/R. Its TOTAL objective is the same normalized lower
objective, but the separate affine tilt is 1/R, not 1. C4 does not claim
componentwise normalization equals the old tilt-one witness. The tests
compare against the old exact primal mirror with this correct tilt.
