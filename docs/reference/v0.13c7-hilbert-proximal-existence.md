## Mathematical increment

C6 proved automatic proximal existence for whole-space real-valued convex
functions in finite dimension, and supplied a conditional canonical iteration
in general spaces. C7 proves existence on a real Hilbert space when the
real-valued convex function h is explicitly continuous. It feeds that result
into the same C6 HasProx proposition, proxMap, and recursive iterate.

Finite-dimensionality is absent from the new Hilbert existence and final
upper-theorem assumptions. It is not replaced by compactness of closed balls.
Continuity is not silently inferred for arbitrary infinite-dimensional convex
functions. All old finite-dimensional interfaces remain unchanged and continue
to discharge continuity automatically in their original setting.

## 1. A general compact-free minimization lemma

`StrongMidpointMinimizer.lean` works on a real normed space. Suppose c>0,
Q is bounded below, and for every x,y,

    Q((x+y)/2) <= (Q(x)+Q(y))/2 - c*||x-y||^2.

Set m=inf_x Q(x). Choose u_k with Q(u_k)<m+1/(k+1), using the infimum property.
For p,q>=N, the lower bound at the midpoint, the displayed inequality, and
these two approximate-minimum inequalities give

    c*||u_p-u_q||^2 <= 1/(N+1).

Thus dist(u_p,u_q)<=sqrt(1/(c*(N+1))), whose right side tends to zero.
`cauchySeq_of_midpoint_gap` records this quantitative argument separately;
it requires neither continuity nor completeness. It does require a global
lower bound, not merely bounds on the listed sequence values.

With CompleteSpace and Continuous Q, the sequence has a limit z and Q(z)=m.
`exists_minimizer_of_midpoint_gap` concludes `exists z, forall y, Q z <= Q y`.
There is no compactness, reflexive weak topology, extraction of a compact
subsequence, or new existence axiom in this argument. The positive midpoint
gap is essential: boundedness alone would not make an orthonormal sequence
Cauchy. The result is a mathematical existence proof, not an executable
minimization algorithm.

## 2. Continuity, a radial lower bound, and the prox objective

`ProximalHilbertExistence.lean` first proves a local-to-global bound without
completeness or finite dimension. If r>0 and h(y)<=h(v)+M whenever
||y-v||<=r, convexity implies

    h(x) >= h(v) - (M/r)*||x-v||.

For x!=v let d=||x-v|| and y=v-(r/d)(x-v). Then ||y-v||=r and
v=(r/(d+r))*x+(d/(d+r))*y. Convexity and the local upper bound give the
result after clearing positive denominators. The x=v case is separate and
never divides by zero.

Continuity at v, with value tolerance 1 and ball radius half the continuity
radius, supplies a suitable local upper bound. Consequently, there is C>=0
with h(x)>=h(v)-C||x-v|| for every x. No supporting subgradient or lower bound
on h itself is assumed.

The auxiliary scalar expression

    hilbertProxObjective L h v x = h(x)+(L/2)*||x-v||^2

is exactly the norm form of C4's original IsProxPoint, not a new prox predicate.
For L>0, completing the square gives the global lower bound

    Q_v(x) >= h(v)-C^2/(2L).

Convexity of h and the inner-product norm identity give

    Q_v((x+y)/2) <= (Q_v(x)+Q_v(y))/2 - (L/8)*||x-y||^2.

Apply the generic lemma with c=L/8 and then the old `isProxPoint_iff_norm`.
`exists_isProxPoint_hilbert` and `existsUnique_isProxPoint_hilbert` conclude
existence and uniqueness for the original predicate. Uniqueness is reused
from C5, not reproved by another semantics.

The assumptions are positive L, convex h, Continuous h, and CompleteSpace E.
No composite minimizer is needed for prox existence. h may itself be unbounded
below. Zero-dimensional spaces are allowed. No L=0 existence/uniqueness claim
is made.

## 3. Reuse of the canonical map, algorithm and exact rate

`ProximalGradientHilbert.lean` defines `hasProx_of_continuous` from the above
result and `hilbertIterate` as the old `iterate` with that proof supplied.
There is no new recursive equation, no alternative oracle, and no default
point when a minimizer is missing. The operator remains noncomputable choice
from a proved nonempty solution set; it is not an executable numerical solver.

Every finite prefix satisfies the old physical IsSmoothProxRun predicate.
`run_eq_hilbertIterate` identifies any supplied same-initial-value run on its
proved prefix. `exists_all_horizons_hilbert` supplies one infinite sequence
whose every finite prefix is a run. The choice is independent of horizon.

`hilbertIterate_eq_finiteDimensional` proves equality with C6's entire finite-
dimensional trajectory. That compatibility theorem alone uses finite dimension;
it does not enter the proof of the new Hilbert existence theorem.

The generic `certificate_upper_hilbert` keeps all three certificate guards:
accepted original Gram checker, empty implicit interpTerms, and exact objective
matching. The rate wrappers call the old C6 results, and hence the unchanged
C5/C4/C2 actual semantics and C3B accepted rational certificate. No multiplier,
rank-one factor, value ordering, assumption ordering or checker is modified.

For T>0, L>0, R>=0, L-smooth convex actual f/g, continuous convex real-valued h,
an ordinary global minimizer xStar of f+h, and ||x0-xStar||^2<=R^2,

    (f+h)(hilbertIterate(T))-(f+h)(xStar) <= L*R^2/(4*T).

The final caller does not supply HasProx, a trajectory, a subgradient certificate,
or Gram data. The exact-radius corollary uses R=||x0-xStar||. Starting at a
composite minimizer remains stationary, including nonzero component gradients
which cancel. No division by R is added.

## 4. Exact lower and whole-value-set compatibility

`witness_nonsmooth_continuous` proves continuity of the old scaled witness
without changing its definition. `hilbertIterate_witness` transports the C6
witness equality only on k<=n+1. It does not incorrectly continue the witness's
linear formula forever.

`oneDimensionalHilbertValues` quantifies actual f,h,g, the reference point and
initial point, positive L, convexity and continuity, and uses hilbertIterate.
It has no existential HasProx proof and no freely supplied run. Under L>0 it
is proved equal to C6's `oneDimensionalValues`, for every real R. The reverse
direction derives continuity on the real line from whole-space convexity and
uses the original run uniqueness theorem. Thus the new description does not
silently enlarge or shrink the one-dimensional problem.

`hilbert_tight_all` transfers the old IsGreatest for R>=0:

    L*R^2 / (4*(n+1)).

The compiler convention n+1 steps is unchanged; the upper API also accepts
actual step count T. This is not a new numerical rate or a cross-universe
supremum definition. General-Hilbert upper and one-dimensional exact attainment
are stated as separate interfaces.
